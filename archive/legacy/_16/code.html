قصة الطابع (The Story of the Stamp)

Issued merely days after the formal declaration of independence, this stamp represents the first sovereign postal emission of the Algerian Republic. It was a monumental logistical and artistic achievement, designed in secret and printed rapidly to replace colonial issues. The stamp served not just as postage, but as a tangible piece of freedom that citizens could hold in their hands, signaling to the world the birth of a new nation.

الوصف (Description)

The design features a central motif of a breaking chain, a universal symbol of liberation. Surmounting the broken links is the crescent and star, echoing the national flag. The background utilizes a subtle gradient meant to evoke the sunrise over the Atlas Mountains. The typography is entirely in Arabic script—a deliberate departure from dual-language colonial issues—proclaiming "République Algérienne" in classical calligraphy.

info

Philatelic Notes

This specific issue is known for several minor printing varieties due to the haste of its production. Look closely at the star's points; in roughly 1 in 500 sheets, the top point is noticeably blunted (Catalog: DZ-001962a). Mint condition examples with perfect centering are increasingly rare. The "+2 F" surcharge was instituted as a mandatory contribution to a national solidarity fund for war orphans.
